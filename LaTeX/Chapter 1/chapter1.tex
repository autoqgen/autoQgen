\section{1.1 Introduction}

Educational assessment is not limited to the production of individual questions. It depends on the organization of academic content, the governance of review and approval, and the careful assembly of selected items into a coherent examination paper. In many institutions, these tasks are distributed across disconnected tools and manual procedures, creating fragmentation between question authoring, curriculum classification, content validation, and assessment preparation. This fragmentation makes it harder to maintain consistent question quality, track review status, and reuse approved materials effectively \cite{mislevy2003ecd,kurdi2020}. The broader challenge is therefore not only question creation, but the reliable coordination of a question bank with the paper-construction workflow that follows it.

AutoQgen addresses this problem by combining taxonomy-based question management, AI-assisted authoring support, review and approval workflows, and configurable paper-generation capabilities within a single web application. The system allows users to create, import, categorize, review, and organize questions; it also supports the assembly of papers from approved content using either manual selection or rule-driven generation. Human oversight remains central: generated candidates are treated as draft material for validation and review rather than as automatically approved assessment items. This aligns with established recommendations for maintaining user control in human-AI workflows \cite{amershi2019} and with the requirement that assessment content should be reviewed before use.

The project is designed around organization-aware access and academic context. Questions are linked to categories, subjects, chapters, and related metadata; users operate within defined organization and permission scopes; and assessment papers can be previewed, designed, exported, and printed as student or teacher copies. This makes AutoQgen relevant to the broader landscape of digital assessment systems, where question banks, content governance, paper generation, and export workflows are increasingly integrated in digital platforms \cite{moodleQuestionBank,moodleQuestionCategories,imsQti3}.

This chapter sets out the background, problem, motivation, objectives, and scope of the system. It explains why automated support for question management and assessment preparation is useful, and how AutoQgen is positioned as an integrated platform rather than a single-purpose generation tool.

\section{1.2 Background of Automated Question and Assessment Systems}

The history of automated assessment systems is closely connected to the development of question banks, item repositories, and test-assembly methods. In traditional educational practice, question records are often managed as reusable items with metadata such as subject, topic, difficulty, marks, answer key, and review status. A well-structured question bank supports retrieval, classification, and selection across multiple assessments; it also enables instructors to reuse valid content while preserving curriculum alignment and accountability \cite{moodleQuestionBank,moodleQuestionCategories,imsQti3}. This basic function remains important, even in modern digital systems, because a question is useful not only by its wording but also by the academic context in which it is assigned and reused.

Research on automated question generation (AQG) has expanded the scope of question management beyond static content repositories. AQG studies examine how textual or structured input can be transformed into candidate questions for education and assessment. Literature in this area includes rule-based systems, statistical ranking approaches, and neural question-generation methods, each of which differs in controllability, linguistic flexibility, and dependence on training data \cite{mitkovHa2003,hoshinoNakagawa2005,heilmanSmith2010,du2017,kurdi2020}. These approaches demonstrate that automated generation can produce useful candidate questions, but they also show that generated material requires validation, review, and contextual alignment before it is suitable for assessment use.

A second stream of related work concerns question quality and redundancy control. Exact duplicate detection reduces repeated content within a bank, while semantic similarity analysis helps identify near-duplicate or conceptually overlapping items. In AutoQgen, duplicate detection and within-paper similarity checking are treated as separate but related functions: exact or normalized duplicate detection is used during authoring and import, while semantic similarity review is used within a paper to surface potential overlaps between questions before finalization \cite{reimers2019sbert,agirre2012sts}. This reflects a broader recognition that content quality is not determined by generation alone; it depends on checking, filtering, and user judgment.

Assessment preparation also requires systematic paper assembly. Classical test-assembly and test-design research frames test construction as a constrained selection problem with requirements on item coverage, difficulty, distribution, and format \cite{vanDerLinden2005,luo2020}. In practice, institutions also need operational features beyond optimization: question availability, approval status, previous-use constraints, chapter coverage, marks allocation, and export-ready formatting. AutoQgen incorporates these practical concerns into its paper-generation workflow by combining eligibility filtering with user-configurable selection preferences and additional assessment-specific constraints.

Finally, secure and role-aware access is central to any system that manages educational content and assessment material. Role-based access control (RBAC) has long been used to separate administrative, authoring, review, and user responsibilities, and this is especially important when content is shared across organizations and teams \cite{sandhu1996rbac,owaspAccessControl}. AutoQgen adopts a layered approach in which global account roles and organization-specific roles operate in parallel, enabling platform administration and organization-scoped content operations to be managed separately while preserving accountability.

\section{1.3 Problem Statement}

A major challenge in educational assessment is not merely writing questions, but maintaining a reliable and reusable pipeline from question creation to paper assembly. In real academic workflows, question records may be authored by different users, stored under inconsistent taxonomy, and reviewed at different times. Without a consistent academic structure and review process, it becomes difficult to determine whether a question is approved, relevant to a subject, suitable for a particular assessment, or already used in a prior paper. These problems are especially visible in organizations that manage large question banks across multiple classes, subjects, and curriculum units.

The problem is intensified by the need to combine question authoring, validation, and review with paper preparation. Manual paper assembly often requires repeated searching, filtering, checking marks, considering chapter coverage, and verifying that selected questions are still eligible. An automated or rule-driven workflow can reduce this effort, but only when it operates on approved or otherwise valid content and can clearly communicate when a specification cannot be met. Admission-oriented papers add another layer of complexity because subject distribution must be allocated according to configurable percentages while preserving validity and academic coverage.

A second issue is content governance. AI-generated candidates can support faster draft creation, but they are not automatically equivalent to reviewed assessment material. Without controlled review, imported or generated questions may carry errors, duplicates, or ambiguities that reduce assessment quality. Similarity review is therefore necessary not as a substitute for judgment, but as a decision-support mechanism for identifying potential overlaps within the same paper \cite{reimers2019sbert,agirre2012sts}. This distinguishes a usable assessment system from a raw content repository.

The aim of AutoQgen is therefore to address the practical coordination of question organization, review, reuse, and paper preparation in a single system. The project does not claim to solve every limitation of educational assessment, nor does it claim measured gains in learning outcomes or efficiency. Its focus is the integration of these workflows into a coherent and controlled application that supports staff decisions while preserving content governance.

\section{1.4 Motivation}

The motivation for AutoQgen is fundamentally practical. Educational institutions need a way to organize a question bank, maintain academic metadata, control review states, and assemble papers from approved content without repeatedly redoing the same tasks in fragmented tools. When these activities are disconnected, the result is lost context, inconsistent review steps, and inefficient reuse of legitimate assessment materials. AutoQgen was developed to reduce this fragmentation by providing a unified environment for question management and paper preparation.

AI-assisted generation was also incorporated because it offers a way to obtain draft question candidates more quickly. However, the system does not treat generated content as automatically approved. Instead, the workflow requires validation, review, and user selection before a generated item becomes part of the bank. This preserves human oversight and keeps the project aligned with responsible AI use in educational contexts \cite{amershi2019}.

The paper-generation features provide a closely related motivation. A question bank is most useful when it can support both manual paper construction and structured, repeatable generation processes. Previous-use controls, subject and chapter distribution, review-state filtering, and paper similarity review are all designed to support users in preparing a valid and manageable assessment from a trusted pool of content. Admission papers extend this pattern to percentage-based allocation across subjects, reflecting a common real-world assessment requirement.

The project therefore responds to a concrete software problem: how to connect question authoring, organization-aware access, and assessment preparation in one platform while preserving review and institutional boundaries. The value lies in the integrated workflow and controlled governance it provides, not in a theoretical claim that it automatically improves educational outcomes.

\section{1.5 Objectives}

\textbf{Main Objective:} To develop an integrated question and assessment management system that supports organized question authoring, controlled review, and configurable examination-paper preparation within an organization-aware environment.

The supporting objectives of the project are as follows:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item To maintain a centralized, taxonomy-linked question bank that records academic placement, metadata, review status, and question content in a structured form.
    \item To support question creation, editing, import, validation, and duplicate checking so that new questions enter the system with consistent quality controls.
    \item To support AI-assisted draft generation and creative-question workflows that produce candidates for human inspection, selection, and review before inclusion in the approved bank.
    \item To provide a review and approval lifecycle for questions, including draft, pending, approved, and rejected states with clearly defined permission checks.
    \item To enable manual and rule-driven paper construction from eligible content, including previous-use controls, chapter targeting, and admission-oriented subject allocation.
    \item To provide secure organization-aware access control and export-ready paper presentation through preview, PDF, DOCX, and browser-print workflows.
\end{enumerate}

\section{1.6 Scope of the System}

The implemented scope of AutoQgen covers the question and assessment workflow represented by its current application features. It includes account access and profile management, organization membership and team structure, academic taxonomy, question authoring and import, review and approval processes, AI-supported draft generation, duplicate detection, similarity review, and paper generation. It also includes paper design, preview, and export functions for student and teacher copies in PDF and DOCX formats, together with browser-based printing.

The system is built using a Next.js web application architecture with React, TypeScript, MongoDB, and Mongoose. The question-generation path uses an Ollama-compatible API, while similarity-related embedding operations use Gemini-based processing and semantic evaluation. These integrations are configurable and may be unavailable when not deployed or configured; the application does not assume that external AI services are always active. Generated content is not automatically approved, and question bank use is restricted by review state, organization scope, and applicable permissions.

The scope of the system also includes operational constraints that are reflected in the implementation rather than assumed in theory. Automatic paper generation uses eligibility rules based on active status, approval status, taxonomy alignment, and organization context. Difficulty, chapter distribution, and question-type preferences are treated as guidance within a selection process, and user-visible warnings are used when a requested target cannot be satisfied exactly. Similarly, the similarity feature is limited to within-paper analysis rather than global semantic duplication across the entire bank. These are important scope boundaries because they define what the system implements and what it does not claim to provide.

\section{1.7 Organization of the Thesis}

This thesis is organized to follow the progression from foundational background to implementation and evaluation. Chapter 2 reviews the related literature and technical background relevant to digital question banks, assessment workflows, automated question generation, similarity analysis, and role-aware assessment systems. Chapter 3 presents the system requirements and design, including the architecture, data model, and user-facing operational flow. Chapter 4 describes the implemented modules and the main technical components of the application. Chapter 5 addresses testing, evaluation, and validation, distinguishing implemented behavior from empirical claims that require further study. The structure of the thesis therefore reflects the practical sequence of the project: background, design, implementation, and evaluation.

\section{1.8 Summary}

This chapter established the educational and technical context for AutoQgen as a question and assessment management system. It identified the key problem of fragmented question management and paper preparation, explained the importance of review and academic context, and positioned AI-assisted generation as a supportive but controlled workflow rather than a replacement for human oversight. The chapter also defined the project’s main objective, its supporting goals, and the practical scope of the implemented system.

AutoQgen is best understood as an integrated platform for authoring, organizing, reviewing, and reusing questions within an organization-aware environment, while also supporting the configuration and export of assessment papers. The following chapters build on this foundation by examining related work, outlining the design, and detailing the implemented system and validation evidence.
